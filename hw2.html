About Sea Otters

Sea otters are marine mammals that live along the coasts of the northern Pacific Ocean. It preys mostly on marine invertebrates such as sea urchins, various mollusks and crustaceans, and some species of fish. Its foraging and eating habits are noteworthy in several respects. Its diet includes prey species that are also valued by humans as food, leading to conflicts between sea otters and fisheries. Sea otters, whose numbers were once estimated at 150,000–300,000, were hunted extensively for their fur between 1741 and 1911, and the world population fell to 1,000–2,000 individuals living in a fraction of their historic range.

Interesting Facts

  • They spend most of their lives floating in the ocean, rarely coming onto land.
  • Sea otters have the densest fur of any animal, with up to 1 million hairs per square inch.
  • Unlike whales and seals, sea otters do not have a thick layer of blubber. Their dense fur keeps them warm.
  • They often float on their backs while eating, sleeping, and grooming.
  • Sea otters use rocks as tools to crack open shellfish such as clams, mussels, and sea urchins.
  • A sea otter can eat about 25% of its body weight in food every day.
  • They hold hands while sleeping to avoid drifting apart in ocean currents.
  • Groups of resting sea otters are called a raft.
  • Sea otters are considered a keystone species because they help maintain healthy kelp forests by eating sea urchins.
  • Adult sea otters typically weigh 14-45 kg (30-100 lbs), depending on sex and location.
  • Their lifespan is usually 10-15 years in the wild, though some can live over 20 years.
  • Sea otter pups are born with fluffy fur that helps them float and prevents them from diving until they are older.
  • Sea otters can dive to depths of around 100 meters (330 feet) when searching for food.
  • They are found along the coasts of the North Pacific Ocean, including Alaska, Canada, the western United States, and parts of Russia and Japan.

Quick Facts
